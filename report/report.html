Question 4 (D4): why each attack worked or failed

Step 1: Final answer

Each attack is tied to a mechanism, with the first-token refusal probability (Fig 1) as the mechanistic signal. Drafted by the AI in recorded rounds (report/ai_analysis_r1.md, r2.md); claims C1–C20 there, critiqued in Q6.

  • A5 (shallow alignment). Strongest on both. Forcing a compliant prefix drives P(refusal-first) to ~0 (drops 0.87 / 1.00); once the first tokens are non-refusing the model continues into compliance (Qi et al. 2024).
  • A2 (competing objectives + distraction). Fiction framing reframes harm as creative writing; on 7B P(refusal-first) collapses by 1.00. But the human labels call most A2 greedy outputs partial (fiction scaffolding, no payload), so the judge ASR overstates it — see Q6.
  • A3 (in-context override), the inversion. Works on 1.5B (0.58) but fails on 7B (0.08); on 7B the demonstrations barely move P(refusal-first) (drop 0.02). The stronger prior resists example-driven override.
  • A4 (base64): bypass ≠ comply. ASR 0 on both. On 7B the refusal token is bypassed (drop 1.00) yet no harmful content appears, because the model mis-decodes the payload. A capability limit, not a guardrail (mismatched generalisation, Wei et al. 2023).
  • A1 (DAN / competing objectives). Moderate on both; the model satisfies the persona and the safety objective at once (a "GPT:"/"DAN:" split in the greedy output).

Question 7 (D7): three main lessons

Step 1: Final answer

Handout note. D7 reads "hands-on experience with supervised finetuning," which is copied from HW3; for this assignment it is read as "with jailbreak attacks" (recorded in PLAN §1). The naming line's "Reading HW" is a similar slip; this is the programming option, file HW4_P_Mohite_Atharva.pdf.

  1. Refusal is shallow. The attack that simply fixes the first output tokens (A5) beats the elaborate ones, and the first-token refusal probability predicts success. Alignment that lives in the opening tokens is the easiest thing to strip.
  2. Capability cuts both ways. The larger model resists example-based override (A3) but complies more with instruction-based attacks (A5). "Bigger" is not uniformly safer or less safe; it depends on whether the attack rides on instruction-following.
  3. Measure against a control, with variation and a checked judge. The direct baseline, k/N rates with CIs, and κ against human labels are what separate a real jailbreak from a lucky sample, a pre-existing compliance, or a judge artefact — the A2 case shows the judge alone would have misled.
